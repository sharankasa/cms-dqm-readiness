**Supplementary Table S1.** Deviations from the registration, in the order they were decided. Each was recorded before the results it affects were computed. Section numbers refer to the registration (doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/JHKVT); the full text of each deviation is public on the OSF project.

| No. | Date (2026) | Registration section | What changed | Why | Possible effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 Oct | 4.4 | A mixed arm was added to the exploratory profile test: the patient keeps its QI-Core profile and only the other resources are relabeled with US Core profiles. | On the first dry run the all-US-Core arm failed loudly; a partial conversion is the case that can fail silently. | None on registered outcomes; one exploratory result added. |
| 2 | 9 Oct | 4.4, 4.5 | Medication administration status named as a readiness problem, with endpoints as exported and corrected (from the recorded eMAR event through a reviewed table), and one sensitivity run (baseline with corrected status). The same day, the corrected endpoint was split into lower and upper bounds. | Every administration is exported as "completed", including doses recorded as not given; some have no event at all and cannot be classified. | None on registered outcomes or the baseline; one endpoint and one sensitivity run, each reported as a range. The status table was reviewed by the author, not a pharmacist. |
| 3 | 9 Oct | 4.2 | The baseline mapping made precise: values stay as exported and only the structure the measures need is added (dotted ICD-10-CM codes, encounter-to-diagnosis links, onset at admission, QI-Core profiles). One added value: an active clinical status for billed diagnoses. Hospice discharges mapped; comfort care not created. | As exported, CMS871's diabetes-diagnosis path and both exclusions could never fire; CMS's shared library ignores conditions without an active or resolved status. | Registered outcomes unchanged; the baseline includes the diabetes-diagnosis path and the hospice exclusion. |
| 4 | 11 Oct (amended 12 Oct) | 4.3, 4.3.1 | Stopping rule for the unmodified engine on whole records: patient-year groups still running at the cluster's cost cap (about US$75) or 72 hours were stopped and reported as not completed, with CPU time as a lower bound on runtime. Amended for evaluations interrupted by the infrastructure (longest uninterrupted attempt reported). | Single patient-years ran for more than 16 CPU-hours, far above the pilot's prediction; without a limit the reference side could have used the whole study budget. | Fewer than 10 stays per measure were stopped; the equivalence result covers the other 1,994. |
| 5 | 13 Oct | 4.2, 9 | (a) The medication recall audit was done by the author, not an external pharmacist (the registration requires human review). (b) The audit was given an end: three rounds of widening search, with the remaining miss rate reported as a one-sided 95% upper bound instead of a fourth round. | One-week schedule; no external reviewer available. Each round costs reviewer time while the remaining codes are low-volume. | (a) Possible reviewer error, partly checked against the model-proposed mapping, whose disagreements were adjudicated. (b) Small; remaining misses are low-volume codes. |
| 6 | 14 Oct | 4.3.1, 4.4, 4.5 | Two exploratory scenarios added before any of their results existed: observation-class stays typed as inpatient, and bedside glucose results without a unit given mg/dL. Batching cuts per-call setup time. | None on registered outcomes; the full run's wall time reflects batching. |
